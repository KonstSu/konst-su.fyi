Facility · Neutrinos

IceCube Neutrino Observatory (with Upgrade and Gen2)

Cherenkov neutrino telescope in a cubic kilometre of ice

Status
Operating
Location
South Pole, Antarctica
Operator
IceCube Collaboration, NSF, UW–Madison
Next
First IceCube Upgrade data; Gen2 in planning

Summary

A cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice instrumented with about 5,000 optical modules. It discovered high-energy astrophysical neutrinos (2013) and the first sources (TXS 0506+056, NGC 1068, the Galactic plane).

Now as of 4 Nov 2026

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Francis Halzen "for his decisive contributions to the creation of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin" (announced 6 October 2026) — Nobel Prize, Physics World. IceCube Upgrade was deployed in the 2025/26 season: construction finished in January 2026 and first data are coming in (UW News, IceCube-Gen2, May 2026). Sources differ on the number of new strings (six or seven).
