Summary

The oldest working spacecraft at Mars: launched on 7 April 2001 and in orbit since 24 October 2001, it marks 25 years in orbit in 2026 (NASA — Odyssey). It maps the composition of the surface and relays data from the rovers.

Now as of 9 Nov 2026

Operating: in October 2026 it was described as the oldest of the working Mars orbiters (The Conversation / New Space Economy, 19 October 2026). Fuel is the main limit. The spacecraft has no fuel gauge; a recalculation in 2023 gave about 4 kg of hydrazine, enough "at least until the end of 2025" (Astronomy, 16 March 2023). In December 2025 Ars Technica wrote that the fuel would probably run out within the next couple of years (Ars Technica). There is no official end date.

Since the loss of MAVEN, Odyssey and MRO carry most of the relay traffic, and scientists say their future depends on NASA's budget (Science News, 5 June 2026).
