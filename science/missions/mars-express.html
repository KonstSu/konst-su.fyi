Summary

ESA's first interplanetary mission to Mars: launched on 2 June 2003 and at Mars since December 2003 (ESA — Mars Express). It studies the planet's atmosphere, surface and interior, and the moon Phobos.

Now as of 9 Nov 2026

Operating. According to ESA, operations are funded until the end of 2026 and an extension to the end of 2029 has been requested (EPSC 2026, C. Wilson). The sources differ here: in 2023 ESA's Science Programme Committee extended the mission to the end of 2026 and gave an indicative extension to 31 December 2028, to be reviewed in 2025–2026 (ESA, 7 March 2023). No decision on 2027–2029 appears in the sources found.

The spacecraft itself could last much longer: there is fuel until at least 2033, and the batteries are compatible with operation until 2042 (EPSC 2026, P. Martin). A software update in May 2025 cut the use of the ageing gyroscopes by 97%; by one estimate the spacecraft could work until 2034 (heise, 19 May 2025).
