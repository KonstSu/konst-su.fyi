Summary

A rover from the company Astrolab with a mass of about half a tonne (480 kg according to MIT Technology Review, 500 kg according to Wikipedia). It is the main payload of Astrobotic's Griffin-1 lander, where it took the place of VIPER. FLIP (FLEX Lunar Innovation Platform) is a test bed for parts of the future crewed rover CLV-1: tyres, batteries, radio and cameras (NASA). It carries 20 science payloads, including a lidar and a helium-3 sensor, and an HPE Spaceborne-4 computer, on which language models are to run on the lunar surface for the first time, analysing images and lidar data (MIT Technology Review, 20 October 2026).

Now as of 13 Nov 2026

Awaiting launch; the dates differ. On 20 October NASA wrote "late 2026" (NASA), MIT Technology Review "no earlier than November 2026" (MIT TR), and Wikipedia, citing NextSpaceflight, "no earlier than January 2027" (Wikipedia). A launch has not been confirmed. According to Wikipedia, the lander finished testing at JPL on 13 October 2026. The rover is expected to work on the surface for about 10 days and is designed to survive at least 150 hours of lunar night (MIT TR). The rocket is a Falcon Heavy from pad LC-39A (Wikipedia).
