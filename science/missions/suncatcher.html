Project Suncatcher

Prototype satellite for AI computing in orbit

Summary

A Google research project to test whether machine-learning accelerators (TPUs) can be moved into orbit, where a solar panel yields up to eight times more energy than on the ground. The idea was published in November 2025 (Google); the prototype satellite was built together with Planet (Google, 24 September 2026).

Now as of 13 Nov 2026

The first response from orbit has been received. The prototype launched on 1 October 2026 as a rideshare payload on the Transporter-18 mission (Google); according to GCN, on a Falcon 9 from Vandenberg into a sun-synchronous orbit (GCN). After several weeks of commissioning, in the week before 30 October, a prompt was sent up asking for a haiku about space; the open model Gemma 4, running on a "data-centre-class" TPU, produced an answer that was received during a pass over Svalbard. Google calls this the first AI response from such an accelerator in orbit; the exact day is not given (Google, 30 October 2026).

Google does not state the number of chips; GCN writes of four. According to GCN, the model runs in sessions of 15 minutes because of thermal limits (other reports say about 20 minutes), and the goal is a year of operation (GCN, 29 October 2026). Next come tests under thermal load and radiation, and in 2027 two satellites with a laser link between them (Google).
