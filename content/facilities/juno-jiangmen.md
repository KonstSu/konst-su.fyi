## Summary
The largest liquid-scintillator detector: 20 kt of scintillator about 53 km from two nuclear power plants. Its main goal is to determine the neutrino mass ordering and to measure oscillation parameters with sub-percent precision. Not to be confused with NASA's [Juno](mission:juno) spacecraft.

## Now
**The first physics result was published in Nature in June 2026, on the cover:** with 59 days of data, the uncertainty on two oscillation parameters shrank by about a third. This is **not** yet a determination of the mass ordering; it shows that the detector and the analysis work ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/12/WS6a2b5c3fa310d6866eb4ddc2.html)). A global fit of existing data gives a slight (~2.2–2.3σ) preference for the normal ordering; that is not a JUNO result ([arXiv:2606.14121](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14121)).
