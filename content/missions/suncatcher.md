## Summary
A Google research project to test whether machine-learning accelerators (TPUs) can be moved into orbit, where a solar panel yields up to eight times more energy than on the ground. The idea was published in November 2025 ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/google-project-suncatcher/)); the prototype satellite was built together with Planet ([Google, 24 September 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/google-project-suncatcher-facts/)).

## Now
The first response from orbit has been received. The prototype launched on 1 October 2026 as a rideshare payload on the Transporter-18 mission ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-satellite-response/)); according to GCN, on a Falcon 9 from Vandenberg into a sun-synchronous orbit ([GCN](https://gcn.com/google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite/22842/)). After several weeks of commissioning, in the week before 30 October, a prompt was sent up asking for a haiku about space; the open model Gemma 4, running on a "data-centre-class" TPU, produced an answer that was received during a pass over Svalbard. Google calls this the first AI response from such an accelerator in orbit; the exact day is not given ([Google, 30 October 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-satellite-response/)).

Google does not state the number of chips; GCN writes of four. According to GCN, the model runs in sessions of 15 minutes because of thermal limits (other reports say about 20 minutes), and the goal is a year of operation ([GCN, 29 October 2026](https://gcn.com/google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite/22842/)). Next come tests under thermal load and radiation, and in 2027 two satellites with a laser link between them ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-satellite-response/)).
