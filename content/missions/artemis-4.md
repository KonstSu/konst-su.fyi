## Summary
After the programme was reshaped, the first crewed Moon landing moved from Artemis III to Artemis IV: four astronauts fly on Orion, and two of them go down to the surface near the south pole for about a week; the spacesuits come from Axiom Space ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iv/)).

## Now
In preparation. The date: "early 2028" on the mission page, last updated on 16 March 2026 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iv/)); at the briefing on 23 October 2026 both Artemis IV and V were described as landings in 2028 ([SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-is-full-steam-ahead-on-artemis-iii-iv-and-v/)). The sources differ on the flight plan: according to the NASA page, Orion docks with the lander in lunar orbit; according to the briefing, a fuelled Starship docks with Orion near Earth and carries it to the Moon (the NASA page is older and probably out of date). Before that, SpaceX has to demonstrate propellant transfer in orbit ("early next year") and an uncrewed landing ("late 2027"); the Axiom spacesuits are behind schedule ([SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-is-full-steam-ahead-on-artemis-iii-iv-and-v/)). No crew has been named.
