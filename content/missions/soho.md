## Summary
A joint solar observatory of ESA and NASA at the L1 point. It was launched on 2 December 1995 for a two-year mission and has worked for more than 30 years; in March 2024 it discovered its 5,000th comet, more than any other observatory in history ([ScienceDaily / ESA, 3 December 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/12/251203084928.htm)).

## Now
Uncertain. Under a 2023 decision, ESA's contribution to SOHO was confirmed until the end of 2025, after which the mission was expected to wind down, with a two-year post-operations phase from 1 January 2026 ([ESA, 7 March 2023](https://sci.esa.int/web/director-desk/-/extended-life-for-esa-s-science-missions)). In December 2025 the Finnish Meteorological Institute, a participant in the mission, wrote that ESA and NASA had extended SOHO "at least into 2026" ([FMI, 5 December 2025](https://en.ilmatieteenlaitos.fi/news/5DrKlReGUyXPoQl7ZsvJc8)). No official decision with a new end date has been found. The SOHO website is still being updated in 2026 ([SOHO](https://soho.nascom.nasa.gov/)).
