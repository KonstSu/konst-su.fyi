## Summary
NASA's rover studying whether the environment in Gale crater was once habitable. It launched on 26 November 2011 and landed on 6 August 2012; since 2014 it has been climbing the slope of Mount Sharp, which is about 5 km high ([NASA — Curiosity](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/), [NASA, 26 August 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/curiosity-reaches-1-kilometer-elevation-gain/)).

## Now
On 26 August 2026 the rover had gained 1 km in elevation on its way from the crater floor up Mount Sharp, which NASA calls the highest point of the 14-year mission ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/curiosity-reaches-1-kilometer-elevation-gain/)). On 29 August 2026 it passed 5,000 sols; by September 2026 it had driven about 37 km ([EarthSky, 24 September 2026](https://earthsky.org/space/curiosity-rover-5000-sols-discoveries-on-mars)).

In April 2026 NASA reported the most diverse set of organic molecules found on Mars so far: 21 carbon-containing compounds in a rock sampled in 2020, seven of them seen on Mars for the first time ([NASA, 21 April 2026](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/mars-science-laboratory/curiosity-rover/nasas-curiosity-finds-organic-molecules-never-seen-before-on-mars/)). In summer 2026 the rover was climbing towards a suspected erosion surface (a "discontinuity") in the sedimentary layers ([mission blog, 11 August 2026](https://science.nasa.gov/blog/curiosity-blog-sols-4968-4974-rock-climbing-towards-the-discontinuity/)).
