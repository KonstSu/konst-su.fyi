## Summary
ESA's planetary-defence mission, a "crash-scene investigation" at Dimorphos, the asteroid moonlet that NASA's DART spacecraft hit in 2022, changing its orbit. Hera will measure its mass, structure and crater so that the kinetic-impactor technique can be modelled precisely.

## Now
The spacecraft is on its final approach. After braking manoeuvres in October its speed relative to the asteroids is about 20 m/s. On 18 October the AFC camera resolved Didymos and Dimorphos as two separate objects for the first time, from 1,280 km away ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Hera/First_light_Hera_resolves_two_asteroids_instead_of_one)). According to [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/ESA-s-Hera-probe-photographs-the-two-asteroids-separately-for-the-first-time-11503411.html), arrival was due on 4 November, and ESA expects to publish the first detailed images of Dimorphos on 25 November. The approach is stepwise: 25–30 km, then 10, 4 and 1 km; the CubeSats will be released "in the coming months".

Sizes: Didymos is 780 m across, Dimorphos 151 m; on average they are 1.15 km apart.
