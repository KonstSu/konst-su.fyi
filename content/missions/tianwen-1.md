## Summary
China's first Mars mission: an orbiter, a lander and the rover Zhurong. It launched on 23 July 2020, entered Mars orbit on 10 February 2021, and Zhurong landed on 14 May 2021 ([The Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/space-missions/tianwen-1)). This page is about the orbiter.

## Now
The orbiter appears to be working, but no official CNSA reports from 2026 have been found. Radio amateurs at AMSAT-DL had not received its signal since 23 December 2025. During the conjunction of Mars with the Sun (December 2025 to January 2026) that is expected, but the signal stayed away long after the conjunction, for unknown reasons ([D. Estévez, 12 March 2026](https://destevez.net/2026/03/where-is-tianwen-1/)). On 17 March 2026 AMSAT-DL received telemetry again: there had been no manoeuvres, and the signal level was normal ([D. Estévez, 18 March 2026](https://destevez.net/2026/03/tianwen-1-received-again-by-amsat-dl/)).

In November 2025 CNSA reported that the orbiter had worked stably for four years and eight months and had imaged the interstellar object 3I/ATLAS from about 30 million km ([China in Space, translation of the CNSA statement](https://www.china-in-space.com/p/tianwen-1-mars-orbiter-performs-closest)). The rover Zhurong went into planned hibernation in May 2022 and did not wake up ([The Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/space-missions/tianwen-1)).
