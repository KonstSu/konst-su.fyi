## Summary
In orbit around Jupiter since 2016, studying its interior, magnetic field and aurorae; the extended mission added flybys of Ganymede, Europa and Io.

## Now
**The status is unclear.** The last officially announced extension ended on 30 September 2025, in the middle of a US government shutdown, and NASA did not confirm at the time whether the spacecraft was still operating ([Space.com](https://space.com/space-exploration/missions/nasas-juno-probe-orbiting-jupiter-may-have-come-to-an-end-but-no-one-can-confirm)). The budget request for fiscal year 2027 again proposes to end Juno ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/the-downlink/shortchanging-nasa-is-simply-not-smart)). Papers based on Juno data keep appearing: in October 2026, one on Jupiter's "shield" of plasma waves and shock regions against the solar wind ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261028232715.htm)) and one on its rippling magnetodisk ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/new-data-from-the-juno-spacecraft-reveals-that-jupiters-has-a-rippling-wavy-magnetodisk)). One source claims an extension to September 2028; I found no official confirmation.
