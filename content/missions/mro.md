## Summary
A NASA orbiter with the high-resolution camera HiRISE and the radar SHARAD, and one of the main relays for the Mars rovers. It launched on 12 August 2005 and has been in Mars orbit since 10 March 2006 ([NASA — MRO](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-reconnaissance-orbiter/)).

## Now
Operating. After the loss of MAVEN (contact broke off in December 2025; the mission was formally closed in June 2026), the surface missions rely on Mars Odyssey and MRO. The rovers now use four relay orbiters, and there are occasional delays. A scientist quoted by Science News says the future of Odyssey and MRO is at risk under NASA's proposed budget ([Science News, 5 June 2026](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/nasa-maven-mars-atmosphere-dead)). The CRISM spectrometer stopped working in 2023; the other instruments are in use ([The Conversation / New Space Economy, 19 October 2026](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/10/19/nasas-mars-orbiter-fleet-is-getting-old-heres-how-that-could-affect-future-exploration/)). Data keep arriving in the PDS archive: release 78 came out on 1 September 2026, and release 80 is scheduled for 1 March 2027 ([PDS Geosciences — MRO](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/missions/mro/)).
