## Summary
Two identical small NASA spacecraft, built by Rocket Lab for the University of California, Berkeley. From two vantage points at the same time, they will study how the solar wind strips away the atmosphere of Mars. They launched on 13 November 2025 on the second flight of New Glenn.

## Now
For a year the pair waited for the planets to line up, on a looping orbit near the Sun–Earth L2 point. Now they fly past Earth and fire their engines close to perigee (an Oberth-effect trans-Mars injection): **Blue on 7 November and Gold on 9 November 2026**, at an altitude of about 970–1,770 km (600–1,100 miles), with burns of about nine minutes. Reported times are around 10:00 UTC on 7 November and 11:00 UTC on 9 November; NASA's own blog post also gives a different time, so treat them as provisional ([NASA blog, 2 November](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/escapade/2026/11/02/nasas-twin-escapade-spacecraft-to-slingshot-past-earth-on-way-to-mars/)).
